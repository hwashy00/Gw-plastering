Ceilings fail in a few predictable ways

Cracks, sagging, water damage and tired old artex, all repairable, and all better dealt with early.

Most ceiling problems in Devon homes come down to one of four things: cracks from movement or failed joints between boards, sagging where old lath-and-plaster has lost its grip on the laths above, water damage from a leak above, or a dated artex finish that's cracking and impossible to patch neatly. Each one has a proper fix, and none of them improves by being left.

We repair ceilings across Exeter and Devon: filling and taping cracks so they stay gone, re-boarding and skimming sagging or damaged areas, and overboarding tired ceilings for a flat, modern finish. Sagging lath-and-plaster in particular is worth acting on — once it starts to come away it only goes one direction, and a partly-collapsed ceiling is a much bigger, messier job than a repaired one.

Repair, overboard, or pull it down?

There's a judgement call on every ceiling job. A sound ceiling with a few cracks just needs repairs. A tired ceiling, or one with artex you want gone, is often best overboarded: new plasterboard fixed straight through into the joists, then skimmed, giving you a brand new ceiling without the mess of demolition. A ceiling that's badly sagged or water-damaged sometimes does need to come down and be re-boarded, and if that's what it needs, we'll say so and quote for it properly.

One thing worth knowing: textured coatings applied before around 2000 can contain asbestos. It's safe when left alone and skimmed over, which is one reason overboarding or skimming is often the sensible route for older artex. Our artex guide covers the options.

Ceiling repair questions

My ceiling is sagging, is it dangerous?
Treat it as urgent. Sagging usually means old lath-and-plaster is losing its grip or boards are coming away, and it only goes one direction. Send us a photo on WhatsApp and we'll tell you how quickly we can look at it.
Can you fix a ceiling without pulling it down?
Often, yes. Cracks can be repaired locally, and tired ceilings can be overboarded, new plasterboard fixed through into the joists and skimmed, which gives you a new ceiling without the demolition mess. Badly sagged or water-damaged ceilings sometimes do need to come down, and we'll tell you which yours is.
There's a crack right across my ceiling, is that serious?
Usually it's a failed joint between plasterboards or normal movement, which is a straightforward repair: rake out, tape and skim. Cracks that keep returning in the same place, or that come with sagging, are worth us looking at properly.
Can you skim over artex on a ceiling?
Usually, yes, and for older artex it's often the sensible route, since textured coatings from before around 2000 can contain asbestos and are safest sealed under a skim or overboard rather than disturbed. We'll advise on the right approach when we see it.

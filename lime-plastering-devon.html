Why old walls need lime

Devon is full of cob, stone and solid-wall properties, cottages and farmhouses that were built to breathe. Using the wrong material on them doesn't just look wrong; it causes real, lasting damage.

Old walls handle moisture differently. Cob and solid stone walls naturally take on and release moisture through the seasons. They were built to breathe, to let damp move through and evaporate away rather than sealing it in. Lime plaster and render work with that, staying breathable so the wall can do its job.

Modern materials trap damp. Seal an old cob or stone wall in hard cement render or gypsum plaster and you trap moisture inside it. That's when you get damp patches, blown plaster, salt staining, rot in timbers and slow damage to the fabric of the building. It's one of the most common, and most expensive, mistakes made on old Devon properties.

Lime is softer and more forgiving. Lime flexes slightly with the natural movement of an old building instead of cracking against it, and it's gentler on original stonework and cob than rigid modern mortars. On the right property, it's not just the traditional choice. It's the correct one.

Lime vs modern plaster

The difference isn't cosmetic. It's about whether your walls stay healthy.

Breathability. Lime is vapour-permeable: moisture passes through and evaporates. Cement and gypsum are far less breathable, so on a solid wall they hold damp in. On a modern cavity-wall house that doesn't matter, on an old solid wall, it's the whole game.

Flexibility. Old buildings move a little, all the time. Lime accommodates that gentle movement; hard cement resists it and cracks, and those cracks then let water in behind the render, the worst of both worlds.

Sympathy to the building. Lime is softer than the cob or stone around it, so any stress goes into the lime rather than the historic fabric. Hard cement is the opposite. It can pull original material away with it when it eventually fails.

None of that means lime is "better" everywhere, on a modern block-built house, a silicone render like K-Rend is the right call. It's about matching the material to the wall. That's the judgement we bring to each job.

How a lime job runs

Lime takes patience and can't be rushed, here's roughly how a job goes.

Look at the wall

We take an honest look at the property's construction and condition, confirms lime is the right choice, and talks you through what's involved. Send a few photos over WhatsApp to get started.

Preparation

Old failed render or loose material carefully taken back, and the wall prepared so the lime has a sound, appropriate surface to key into.

Applied in coats

Lime is built up in coats, with each one given time to cure properly before the next. This can't be hurried, rushing lime is how jobs fail, so the timeline is longer than modern plaster.

Finished & left to cure

The final coat finished to suit the property, then left to fully cure. We'll advise on breathable paints and aftercare so the work lasts.

Lime questions

How do I know if my property needs lime?

As a rule of thumb, older solid-wall properties, cob, stone, anything built before cavity walls became standard, are usually best in lime. If your walls are thick and solid rather than a modern cavity build, lime is very likely the right choice. We'll confirm after looking at the wall.

Why not just use normal plaster or cement on an old wall?

Because modern gypsum plaster and hard cement render are far less breathable. On a solid old wall they trap moisture inside, which leads to damp patches, blown plaster, salt staining and damage to the wall's fabric over time. Lime avoids that by staying breathable.

Is lime more expensive than modern plaster?

It's typically more involved, lime is applied in coats and needs time to cure between them, so a job takes longer than modern plaster and that's reflected in the price. But on the right property it prevents damp problems that are far more costly to put right later. Every job is quoted individually.

My old render is cracked and damp, can lime fix it?

Often, yes. A very common problem on Devon period properties is hard cement render trapping damp in the wall. Taking that off and replacing it with breathable lime lets the wall dry out and can resolve persistent damp. We'll assess whether that's what's going on before recommending anything.

Can you do internal lime plastering as well as external?

Yes. We do both internal lime plastering to walls and ceilings, and external lime rendering. On an old property it often makes sense to keep both breathable for the same reasons.

Lime, or a modern render?

If your walls are cob, stone or solid brick, lime is the answer and modern render will cause you problems. If you're not sure which you've got, our comparison of render types spells out the differences, and why sealing an old wall is such an expensive mistake.

What does lime work cost?

Lime costs more than modern plaster. There's a good reason for that.

It's slower

Lime goes on in several coats with weeks of curing between them. You can't rush it and you can't force it, the work takes as long as it takes.

It's skilled

Far fewer plasterers can work lime properly. It behaves differently to gypsum and it's unforgiving if you get it wrong.

Cheap lime quotes are a warning sign

If a lime quote looks suspiciously low, it's often cement with a bit of lime in it. That will wreck a cob wall, and you won't find out for a couple of years.

The wall dictates the job

Old cob and stone walls are never square, never flat and never the same twice. Some need building back up before anything else happens.

Season matters

Lime doesn't like frost and it doesn't like baking heat. Timing the work right is part of doing it properly.

It's the only right answer

On a listed building or a cob cottage, modern render isn't a cheaper option. It's a mistake that costs far more to undo.

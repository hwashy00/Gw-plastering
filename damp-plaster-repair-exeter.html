About damp & blown plaster

Blown, hollow-sounding or crumbling plaster is usually a symptom — and the damp behind it needs sorting first.

When plaster bubbles, cracks in patches or comes away when you tap it, it's normally moisture and salts breaking the bond between plaster and wall. Older Devon properties with solid walls are especially prone to it. Here's the honest part: skimming straight over damp plaster just fails again. We'll look at what's actually going on — if it's a damp problem that needs treating or a wall that needs to breathe, he'll tell you straight rather than paper over it. Once the wall's sound and dry, he hacks off the blown plaster, re-plasters (using a breathable lime where the wall needs it rather than trapping moisture in), and skims it back to a clean finish that lasts.

Damp & blown plaster questions

Straight answers, no waffle.

How do I know if my plaster is blown?

Tap the wall with your knuckle. Sound plaster gives a solid, dull knock; blown plaster sounds hollow and drummy because it has come away from the wall behind. You will often see bubbling, crumbling or a powdery white bloom on the surface too.

Can you just plaster over damp plaster?

No — and anyone who offers to is storing up a problem for you. Fresh plaster over a damp, blown wall will blow again within months. The old material has to come off, the wall has to dry, and the cause of the damp has to be dealt with first.

Do I need to fix the damp before you plaster?

Yes. Plastering is the last job, not the first. If the damp is coming from a leaking gutter, failed pointing or rising damp, that needs sorting or the new plaster will fail. We will tell you honestly what we can see when we look.

How long does damp plaster need to dry before painting?

Allow roughly 7 to 14 days for new plaster to dry out fully, longer in winter or on a thick coat. It goes from dark brown to a uniform pale pink when it is ready. Use a breathable mist coat first — thinned emulsion — rather than going straight in with a full coat.

What affects the cost of damp plaster repair?

The plastering is the easy part. The damp is the job.

The cause comes first

Be wary of anyone who'll plaster a damp wall without asking where the water's coming from. Fix the leaking gutter, the failed pointing or the bridged damp course first, or the new plaster blows again within a year and you pay twice.

How much has to come off

A small blown patch is a couple of hours. A whole wall hacked back to brick is a day, plus a skip.

Drying time

A wall that's still wet can't be plastered. Sometimes the honest answer is to wait — we'd rather tell you that than take your money twice.

Whether it needs tanking

Below ground or in a persistently damp spot, a waterproof render or tanking coat may be needed before the finish goes on.

Salt contamination

Rising damp brings salts up into the plaster. Those need dealing with properly or they'll come through the new finish as white bloom.

Old walls

Cob and solid stone need lime, not gypsum. Sealing them with modern plaster traps the moisture inside the wall.

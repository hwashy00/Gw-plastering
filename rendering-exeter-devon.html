External rendering across Devon

Render does two jobs at once. It weatherproofs your home and completely transforms how it looks.

Devon throws a lot at a building: driving rain, damp, salt air near the coast and long spells of green-growing weather. A good external render is your home's first line of defence against all of it. We work mainly with modern silicone renders, including K-Rend silicone systems, which are breathable, highly water-repellent and far lower-maintenance than the old sand-and-cement renders that crack and stain.

The big advantage of silicone render is that it's through-coloured, the colour runs all the way through the render rather than being painted on top. That means no repainting every few years, a consistent finish, and a wide range of colours to suit anything from a modern new-build to a traditional Devon property. Whether it's a full house, a single elevation, or repairs to existing render, the aim is the same: a clean, even, weatherproof finish that lasts.

Why silicone render suits Devon homes

Two things matter most in a wet, coastal county: keeping water out, and stopping the green algae and staining that thrive in damp air. Silicone render is water-repellent so rain beads and runs off rather than soaking in, but it's also breathable, so any moisture in the wall can still escape, which is exactly what you want on Devon's older and exposed properties.

It's also low-maintenance and self-cleaning to a degree, holds its colour, and comes in a big range of shades. One thing worth knowing: render needs the right weather to go on, ideally between about 5°C and 35°C, with protection from rain and frost for the first couple of days while it cures. We'll plan the job around the forecast so it goes on right and lasts.

Plastering questions

Do I need to repaint silicone render?
No, silicone render is through-coloured, meaning the colour runs all the way through rather than sitting on top as paint. That's one of its biggest advantages: no repainting every few years, and no patchy fading.
Can you render over old pebbledash or existing render?
Often, yes. It depends on the condition of what's already there. If the existing surface is sound it can usually be rendered over after the right preparation; if it's blown or loose it may need taking back first. We'll tell you which after a look.
Does render help with damp?
Silicone render is water-repellent and breathable, so it sheds rain while still letting the wall release moisture. It's a strong weatherproofing layer, but it isn't a cure for rising damp or an internal leak, those need sorting at the source.
How long does external rendering take?
It depends on the size of the property and the weather. A typical house is usually a few days' work, but render needs dry, mild conditions to go on and cure, so we plan around the forecast rather than rushing it.

What does rendering a house cost in Devon?

Rendering is priced by the square metre, but the metre rate isn't the whole story.

Which system

Silicone and K-Rend cost more per m² than traditional sand and cement, but never need painting. Lime is dearer again, and the only option on old walls.

Scaffolding

On anything above single storey you're paying for scaffold. It's often one of the biggest line items on a render quote.

The existing wall

Rendering over sound existing render is one job. Hacking a house back to brick first is a completely different one.

Access

A detached house with a driveway is straightforward. A terrace with no side access and a narrow lane is not.

Detailing

Bell casts, beading, window reveals, quoins and sills all take time and skill. A plain elevation is quicker than a fussy one.

Size of the property

Obvious, but it's measured in square metres of wall, not bedrooms. A long bungalow can have more wall than a narrow three-storey.

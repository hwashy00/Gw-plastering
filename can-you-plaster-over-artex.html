Plastering guide

Can you plaster over artex?

Short answer: usually yes. Most artex ceilings can be skimmed straight over, and that is nearly always the better option.

In most cases, yes. You can skim straight over artex. It is quicker, cleaner, cheaper and far less disruptive than scraping it all off. Nine times out of ten, that is exactly what we do.

The reason people assume it has to come off is that removal used to be the standard approach, and there are still plenty of tradesmen who will quote you for it without asking whether it is necessary. It usually isn't.

When you can skim straight over it

Which is most of the time.

The pattern is light

A fine stipple or a light swirl can be bonded and skimmed over without any drama. This covers the majority of artex ceilings we see.

The surface is sound

If the artex is firmly stuck down and the plaster underneath is solid, there is nothing to gain from removing it.

It isn't flaking

Artex that is intact and dry takes a bonding coat perfectly well.

When it has to come off

Less common, but worth knowing.

Very heavy peaks

Deep swirls and sharp peaks stand too proud to skim over cleanly. They need knocking back first, or taking off entirely.

It's blown or loose

If it sounds hollow or lifts when you press it, skimming over just puts a nice new surface on top of a failing one.

Water damage underneath

If the ceiling has been leaked on, the board may have gone. Skimming over that is pointless. It needs cutting out.

The asbestos rule, read this bit

Artex applied before 2000 may contain asbestos. Chrysotile asbestos was used as a binder in textured coatings in the UK right up until it was banned in 1999, so any artex in an older property should be treated as suspect until it is tested.

Here is the important part: artex containing asbestos is not dangerous while it is intact. The fibres are bound into the coating. It becomes a risk when you disturb it, sanding, scraping, drilling or dry-scraping releases fibres into the air.

That is another reason skimming over is often the better answer. Bonding and skimming over the surface does not disturb it. We never sand or scrape artex in a pre-2000 property without a test first, and nor should anyone else.

Common questions

The ones we get asked most.

Can you plaster over artex?

Yes, in most cases artex can be skimmed straight over without removing it. The surface is bonded and then skimmed to a flat, paint-ready finish. This is quicker, cleaner and cheaper than removal, and it avoids disturbing the artex, which matters in older properties where it may contain asbestos.

When does artex need removing rather than skimming?

Artex needs removing when the pattern has very heavy peaks that stand too proud to skim over cleanly, when it is blown or loose and sounds hollow when tapped, or when the plasterboard underneath has been water damaged and needs replacing anyway.

Does artex contain asbestos?

Artex applied before 2000 may contain chrysotile asbestos, which was used as a binder in textured coatings in the UK until it was banned in 1999. It is not dangerous while intact, the risk comes from disturbing it by sanding, scraping or drilling. Any artex in a pre-2000 property should be tested before it is disturbed.

Is it cheaper to skim over artex or remove it?

Skimming over is significantly cheaper because it is quicker, creates far less mess and waste, and avoids the need for asbestos testing and controlled removal. Removal only makes sense where the artex is unsound or the pattern is too heavy to cover.

How long does it take to skim an artex ceiling?

An average artex ceiling is usually bonded and skimmed in a single day. It then needs roughly 7 to 14 days to dry fully before it can be painted.

About water-damaged ceilings

A water-stained, bulging ceiling only gets worse if it's left — and a sagging one can eventually come down.

Whether it's a burst pipe, an overflowing bath, a leaking shower tray or a slipped roof tile, once water gets into plasterboard it stains, blows and starts to sag. Painting over it never works — the damage is in the board, not on the surface. We cut out the affected area, re-boards it, and skims it back to a smooth, paint-ready finish that matches the rest of the ceiling. One honest bit of advice up front: get the leak itself fixed first. There's no point making good a ceiling while water's still coming through — sort the source, then we make it disappear.

How we sort a water-damaged ceiling

Done properly, not painted over.

Assess & make safe

We check how far the damage has spread and make sure it's safe — wet plaster near light fittings needs care.

Cut out the damage

The stained, blown plasterboard is cut back to sound, dry material — no guessing, no shortcuts.

Re-board

Fresh plasterboard fitted flush, ready to take a skim that sits level with the existing ceiling.

Skim smooth

Skimmed to a flat, seamless finish that blends in — you won't see where the repair was.

Ready to paint

Left clean and ready for a coat of paint, so the whole ceiling looks like new again.

Honest advice

If it's an ongoing leak or something bigger, we'll tell you straight rather than just cover it up.

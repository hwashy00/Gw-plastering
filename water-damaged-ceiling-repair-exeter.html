About water-damaged ceilings

A water-stained, bulging ceiling only gets worse if it's left, and a sagging one can eventually come down.

Whether it's a burst pipe, an overflowing bath, a leaking shower tray or a slipped roof tile, once water gets into plasterboard it stains, blows and starts to sag. Painting over it never works, the damage is in the board, not on the surface. We cut out the affected area, re-boards it, and skims it back to a smooth, paint-ready finish that matches the rest of the ceiling. One honest bit of advice up front: get the leak itself fixed first. There's no point making good a ceiling while water's still coming through, sort the source, then we make it disappear.

Water damaged ceiling questions

Straight answers, no waffle.

Will a stained ceiling just paint over?

Rarely. Water stains bleed straight back through ordinary emulsion, and if the plaster has been saturated it will have lost its strength. A light stain on sound plaster can be sealed with a stain-block primer, but a sagging, cracked or crumbling ceiling needs the damaged section taking out and re-boarding.

How do I know if the ceiling needs replacing or just repairing?

If it is stained but firm, it is usually a repair. If it is sagging, bulging, cracked around the edges or feels soft when pressed, the board has lost its integrity and that section needs cutting out and replacing. We can tell you which within a few minutes of looking at it.

Do I need to fix the leak first?

Yes, always. There is no point replacing a ceiling under an active leak. Get the leak stopped, let things dry out, then we come in and make good.

How long does a ceiling repair take?

A patch repair is usually a day. A full ceiling, strip, re-board and skim, is typically one to two days for an average room, plus drying time before you can paint.
